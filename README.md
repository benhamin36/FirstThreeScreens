# FirstThreeScreens

## Need, Persona, Capability, Value

- Need: When students have questions, they may want to get help during odd hours when teachers and TAs are not available.
- Persona: Student with a busy work schedule who doesn’t have time to work on assignments except for at odd hours such as late at night or very early in the morning.
- Capability: Find a knowledgeable individual who can help with their assignments when they are having difficulties but don’t have time for the normal hours of teachers and TAs
- Value: Availability of tutoring. Help students find this help at all times during the day.

## Three Screens

- Screen 1: Landing page. Indicate that the app is about getting help, then paying it forward.
- Screen 2: Get help: show a list of students who are knowledgeable in the subject and are free to pay forward whatever help they got.
- Screen 3: Give help: after getting help, show a list of students who are wondering about topics that the user has proven to be useful for.

## Design Question Plan

1. What makes you give up and stop looking for help?
- Prediction: I can’t find anyone that will be able to help me.
- Prototype: Helpers can say they don’t feel confident helping others.
2. What would you need to stop using this to get tutoring?
- Prediction: An app that pays people to help, or my teachers and TAs to have better office hours.
- Prototype: We let students pay it forward. It is currently possible for a student to receive tutoring and not pay it forward. We’re almost certainly never going to have more tutors than those who need tutoring. As a result, it may be difficult to get good tutors in good time.
3. Who else do you know who needs tutoring when professors and TAs are not available?
- Prediction: Most people do. I have tons of friends who have to do homework late at night, and the TAs and professors can’t work with our schedules as much.
- Prototype: The app is usable whenever students are online, so the range of available times are much wider than a small team of TAs or a professor likely will be.
4. I am going to show you the opening screen for five seconds. (Hide it.) What does this product do?
- Prediction: Well, I didn’t have much time to read. Something about getting help? There was like a lined paper look. Is it about school or something? A school help site?
- Prototype: Added the lined paper look to help students get the gist of the academic nature of the site quickly.

## Design Justification and First Read

- Before reading there are hints at academia through the lined-paper graphic, though it doesn't get much more specific.
- There are some extraneous symbols in the diagram showing students helping each other, but other elements communicate valuable things.
- Most pages use the Proximity principle to keep things together, such as the different subjects and the list of students that can help. However, the cards with individual information and the graphic showing the pay-it-forward chain use Common Region grouping.
- The two other pages do their part, and they all have a solid way to return to the landing page, through the home button and other occasional reconnecting links.
- The AI initially struggled to make the landing page have any visual communication of the purpose of the site, which is why I encouraged it to add the lined paper look. I also asked it to add a graphic of students, to help show the individualistic aspects of the site.
